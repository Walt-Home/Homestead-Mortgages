# Defects to plant in synthetic servicing data

Real servicing data is uneven: values present but wrong, flags left off,
histories cut short, files late or malformed. The Sprint Standup of 22 October
2026 asked that our synthetic loans carry intentional errors of that kind, so
an agent trained or tested on them meets the data a subservicer actually hands
over. This page lists 47 such defects. For most it gives public evidence that
the defect happens, or the requirement it breaks; six have none (D-10, D-17,
D-27, D-42, D-43 and D-47). For each it gives how to plant it in a synthetic
transfer or ten-year history, what in the servicing app should catch it, and
whether an environment can grade the handling. It is the companion to
docs/subservicer-data.md, which lists what the data is.

Two findings stand out.

- **The rules of the servicing app's boarding gate catch none of the ten
  defects ranked first below, except D-24 by accident when a planted row
  breaks the history's continuity; later processes catch some.** About a third
  of the rules check that a field is present, and about another third trust a
  flag the transferor sets. The public record (CFPB supervisory findings,
  consent orders and complaints) is mostly about values that are present and
  wrong. Some of the ten are caught later, or would be:
  - an unflagged servicemember, by the military-status (DMDC) check that
    `loan.boarded` arms for every borrower, due within five servicer business
    days (SM_DMDC_VERIFY_BOARDING_0), once an agent runs it; an SCRA case then
    opens only on a loan made before the service began;
  - a bankruptcy with its flag off, by the PACER scrub before a foreclosure
    referral and the presale review; the nightly scrub the spec asks for is
    not built, so no automatic check catches it in collections, though a court
    notice that arrives later records the stay (14.1 notice intake) without
    stopping the late charges, which cashiering still assesses and collects
    (cashiering-18);
  - loss mitigation with its flag off, which the 1.7 in-flight carryover
    verification should catch: an agent's review that works from the
    transferor's files rather than the flag and falls due on the transfer date
    (not reproduced, and with no loss-mitigation file it has nothing to find);
  - a hazard lapse on a loan boarded as non-escrowed, by insurance tracking,
    which the servicing app's engine applies to every boarded loan
    (`apps/servicing/src/domain/insurance/ops-9-1.ts:157-162`); the runtime
    never builds that tracker, only a test does (`9-1.spec.test.ts:53`), so
    no automatic check catches the lapse;
  - a missing mortgage-insurance original value, by the escalation an agent's
    check opens at boarding;
  - outside the ten, a maturity, P&I or modification mismatch of material
    size, by the boarding schedule projection (35.5), in the same boarding
    transaction (D-02, D-05, D-07).
- **The gate has gaps of its own, reproduced against the vendored decoder.**
  Any yes-or-no value but a literal "Y" reads as No, so "Yes", "y" or "1"
  boards a loan as non-escrowed, out of bankruptcy or out of loss mitigation
  (`apps/servicing/src/domain/boarding/tape-codec.ts:49`). The bankruptcy and
  foreclosure details are dropped with their flags. The loss-mitigation
  details are kept; the flag alone stops the case from opening
  (`apps/servicing/src/domain/transfers/ops-1-7.ts:101`). One malformed money
  or date cell throws out of the decoder and aborts the whole batch with no
  `transfer.tape.rejected` message, where the spec wants a rejection that
  lists the failing rows (`tape-codec.ts:48`, `:52`; spec 1-1 :112). A
  malformed count reads as NaN, and `1,234.56` is accepted. A blank "successor
  complete" or "fees itemized" reads as yes (`tape-codec.ts:258`, `:260`).
  `apps/servicing` is vendored and never edited here, so these go to the
  servicing app's own source; until then the generator plants them and the
  environments grade around them.

How often each field is wrong is not public: the injection rates are a design
choice until a subservicer's sample is in hand.

Paths are relative to the repo root. `AS/` means `apps/servicing/` and `ENV/`
means `packages/environments/src/`. "spec 1-1" is

**How far each citation was checked**

- **†** The passage was re-read on 22 October 2026 in the text of the fetched
  PDF.
- **No mark on a public citation:** the source was fetched in research; the
  passage was not re-read.
- **‡** A code or spec citation carried from research and not re-read.
- **Every other `AS/` and `ENV/` citation** was read for this page.

**Two places a defect can go**

1. **The transfer package.** These are the 12 files laid out in
   `AS/fixtures/transfer-batch-demo/LAYOUT.md`, written by our own generator.
   The vendored fixture is never edited.
2. **The ten-year history.** These are rows in the servicing app's tables,
   such as `payments`, `fees`, `escrow_lines`, `disbursements`, `contacts` and
   `mi_policies` (docs/subservicer-data.md, the domain tables).

**How grading works.** Each injection writes a label into the generator's own
manifest: the loan, the defect ID, the file or table, the field, the true
value, the planted value and the expected handling. This copies
`designed_outcomes` (`AS/fixtures/transfer-batch-demo/manifest.json:81-234`).
The detection grader is the existing test pattern: hard failures must match
exactly, and planted warnings must be found while extra ones are allowed
(`AS/src/domain/boarding/demo-batch.test.ts:80-86`). Where a ported engine
exists, the engine grades the money.

## The first ten to inject

Every defect the sample already plants, missing fields and wrong values alike,
is one a gate rule sees, and the gate catches each of them
(`manifest.json:81-234`). Planting more of those only teaches an agent to
replay the rules. The ten below are defects no gate rule sees: values present
but wrong that no rule compares, and flags turned off. The public record is
mostly about this kind of defect. **The boarding gate's rules catch none of
the ten,** except D-24 by accident when a planted row breaks the history's
continuity. Later processes catch some of them, and each item says which.

1. **D-31, an escrowed loan boarded as non-escrowed.** A regulator found this
   (BSI ¶23†). It leads to missed tax and insurance deadlines. Nothing notices
   the escrow flag itself. Insurance tracking would catch a hazard lapse,
   because its engine tracks non-escrowed loans too, but only its test
   constructs it (`AS/src/domain/insurance/9-1.spec.test.ts:53`) and the cycle
   registry has no row for it
   (`AS/src/domain/operations-runtime/cycles.ts:279-326`), so on a boarded loan
   nothing automatic catches the lapse; an agent working the 9.x insurance
   tools could. Unpaid taxes surface only through the tax
   service's delinquency report (spec 3-7 :55). Combined with D-42 it takes a
   one-character injection.
2. **D-35, loss mitigation or a trial plan not flagged.** This is the most
   often cited transfer defect: SH13W†, B14, SH11, NSM, BSI and GT all
   describe it (NSM and GT as allegations). The harm is borrowers wrongly
   pushed toward foreclosure or asked for the full payment. The decoder keeps
   the details, but the flag alone decides whether a case opens, so with it
   off no case opens and no (k) clock is armed (`tape-codec.ts:250`;
   `AS/src/domain/transfers/ops-1-7.ts:101`). The 1.7 in-flight carryover
   verification, which works from the files rather than the flag, should catch
   it by the transfer date.
3. **D-42, a free-text yes or no read as No.** It silently turns escrow,
   bankruptcy and loss mitigation off at the gate (`tape-codec.ts:49`). Our
   partner-tape environment already draws this variety. It is also the
   cheapest way to produce D-31, D-35 and D-37.
4. **D-03, the late-fee cap.** The CFPB alleges Ocwen found it needed more
   than 43,000 corrections to the state-law maximum late fee in its system
   (OCW ¶46†). Separately, CFPB examiners found servicers failed to input
   loan-agreement late-fee caps and charged the state maximum (SH29†). The
   tape cannot carry the cap at all, and the late-charge engine grades the fee
   exactly.
5. **D-01, ARM terms present but wrong.** The CFPB alleges Ocwen found it
   needed more than 24,000 corrections to loans' first rate-cap maximum and
   more than 27,000 to loan terms (OCW ¶46†). The CFPB found BSI failed to
   enter prior servicers' rate-adjustment data for thousands of ARM loans and
   sent statements with the wrong rate (BSI ¶¶45–46). HF-006 checks only that
   the fields exist. In the runtime no boarded ARM is adjusted at all, right
   terms or wrong (arm-15). The arm environment's port grades it.
6. **D-12, an unflagged servicemember.** It is 9.3% of the Independent
   Foreclosure Review's financial-harm errors (IFR). The harm is large,
   because a sale can be void (docs/subservicer-data.md, Outside feeds,
   military status). HF-012 needs the flag the defect removes. `loan.boarded`
   arms a DMDC check of every borrower, due within five servicer business days
   (SM_DMDC_VERIFY_BOARDING_0), but nothing in the runtime runs it: an agent
   records the active-duty status with `dmdc.verify` or `dmdc.results.import`,
   and the batch upload is portal-only
   (`AS/src/infra/integrations/legal.ts:52`). An SCRA case opens only on a
   loan made before the service began (`openScraCase`), so the injection
   should plant one. Even then, the case's late-charge waiver moves the
   ledger but not the fee record, so the charge is still billed (scra-1).
7. **D-37, bankruptcy details present with the flag off.** It is 8.9% of the
   review's harm errors (IFR). The harm is a violation of the automatic stay.
   The decoder drops the details (`tape-codec.ts:248`), and boarding never
   queries PACER. The PACER scrub before a foreclosure referral catches it,
   and the presale review checks that the scrub is clear. The nightly scrub of
   every borrower that the spec asks for is not built, so no automatic check
   catches it in collections; a court notice that arrives later records the
   stay through the 14.1 notice intake, but the cashiering cycle never reads
   it, so late charges are still assessed and collected (cashiering-18).
8. **D-18, mortgage-insurance dates or original value wrong.** Two complaints
   allege it (OCW ¶146†, NSM ¶55), one bulletin names the data (B20 p.9), and
   a supervisory report found PMI not terminated on schedule (SH29 p.13).
   Borrowers pay premiums they no longer owe. W-006 checks only the
   certificate. A missing original value opens an escalation only when an
   agent runs the boarding check (SM_MI_ORIGINAL_VALUE_MISSING_60); a wrong
   one does not. The pmi environment grades it.
9. **D-24 and D-25, history dated before the loan was made, and misapplied
   payments.** OCW ¶41† alleges both. They can corrupt every delinquency
   figure computed from the history (`delinquency.ts:11-12`): an unpaid
   pre-origination row, or a misapplied receipt, changes the oldest-first
   result. D-24 is deterministic to grade. A gate rule (W-014) sees it only
   when a planted row breaks the history's continuity, and then for the wrong
   reason. The vendored sample already carries it, unlabeled, on 16 loans.
   D-25 goes in the ten-year history, where the payments environment's
   allocation engine grades it.
10. **D-15, wrong flood data.** The CFPB alleges that Ocwen's own review found
    inconsistent or incomplete flood determination data loaded for its loans
    (OCW ¶138†) and inaccurate flood-zone data on more than 3,000 loans (OCW
    ¶139†). It also alleges that in one bank's audit of loans where Ocwen had
    force-placed flood insurance, about half already had the borrower's own
    flood policy (OCW ¶137†). W-007 reads only a yes/no flag, and boarding
    does not query the flood vendor.

The runners-up are D-08 (bad addresses), D-43 (one bad cell aborting the whole
batch) and D-23 (fees treated as itemized when the field is blank).

## Gaps and caveats

- **No public source gives how often each field is wrong [NOT FOUND].** The
  only measured rates are aggregates:
  - 72% of the loans Ocwen verified in April 2014, and 90% in March 2016, had
    errors or incomplete information requiring correction (OCW ¶45†; an
    allegation).
  - Total financial-injury error rates at three Federal Reserve-regulated
    servicers, with few reviews complete, were 0.0%, 3.3% and 11.4%. One
    category reached 20.3% (IFR Tables A.4–A.5).
  - Ocwen's fee test failed at 10.90% (NMS). Injection rates are therefore a
    design choice, not a measurement, until a subservicer's sample is in hand
    (docs/subservicer-data.md, What this means for synthetic data).
- **The complaints and the press releases on them (OCW, NSM, OCWPR, GT) are
  allegations.** GT's were settled by order. The consent orders state findings
  that the companies neither admit nor deny, and the supervisory highlights
  report examiners' findings.
- **Not verified:** the ‡ citations; whether inbound insurance documents are
  matched to evidence (D-16); whether boarding knows about trial plans (D-29);
  and New York DFS's letter (D-40). Settled since the first draft: boarding
  queries none of PACER, the flood vendor or the military-status lookup (D-12,
  D-15, D-37), and D-28's effect on delinquency has been run.
